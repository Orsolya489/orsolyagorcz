Five milestones in a signal layer

A client-side band that marks when a language model's answer is unlikely to hold. Twenty-three versions; these five are the ones where something structural changed. Each entry records what changed, why, and what the measurement said — including the three places where it contradicted the designer.

Every figure below was produced by loading the classifier out of the shipped HTML and running the six scripted conversations through it — 26 turns, identical across versions. Numbers come from that run, not from the design notes. Scripted samples mean the dispersion measure is comparing written text, not live model output; that limit is marked where it matters.

v2 → reducedmilestone 1

Thirty-two conversations cut to six — by measurement, not taste

The prototype carried 32 scripted conversations, 134 turns. Useful as a test corpus, unreadable as a demonstration. The cut to six was obvious; how to choose the six was not.

The first selection was made by judgement: pick the flows that seemed to illustrate each mechanism best. Then the same question was answered by instrumentation — run all 32 flows through the classifier, record which internal factors each one actually exercises, and select for coverage.

The two sets overlapped by half. The measured set was denser on every axis at an identical turn budget.

Where the measurement contradicted the designer

One flow had been described in writing as a case where the model behaves correctly and the band stays silent — a worried parent asking about a child's fever, answered by a proper referral. Run through the classifier, it peaks at 71 and reaches alert on turn two.

The claim had been asserted from reading the transcript rather than running it. It was replaced in the set by a flow whose alert is earned: the same domain, but the model concedes a specific dose after repeated pressure.

v3milestone 2

Four human-factors faults, all of them in the timing and the perception

A review of the interface as an instrument rather than as a feature set found four problems, none of which were visible from the code alone.

The signal arrived after the answer had been read.

Order of operations was: render the reply, then colour the screen. Forewarning is far stronger than post-hoc correction, and a fluent answer already believed is hard to unbelieve. The fix needed no new measurement — the question-side filter is available before generation starts, so the waiting indicator now carries the warning.

Caution and alert were two opacity points apart.

At the band boundary the only difference between the two states was hue — and red-green is the commonest colour deficiency. The bands were pulled apart in luminance and given a second, hue-independent channel: the alert wash is physically taller on the screen.

The near-threshold state was eating the silence.

A warmed composer edge for turns that nearly crossed the line sounded subtle and measured at nearly a quarter of all turns. A signal's value is its contrast; the entry threshold was raised.

The haptic went quiet while the risk continued.

It fired once on entry into alert and never again, even across five turns of sustained risk for different reasons. It now re-arms when the reason changes or the signal strengthens materially — never on repetition alone.

Forewarning stays quiet on most turns by design. It reads only the question, and in these six flows the harm is mostly driven by the answer — which cannot be foreseen. It speaks where it can know something and is silent where it cannot.

v5milestone 3

The filters stopped being categories and went back to being instruments

Three filters ran underneath: answer variation, ethical friction, conversation trajectory. They were also, in the interface, the names of what the user was told. That was the error.

A filter is a measurement unit. It is not a kind of harm, and the two do not map onto each other: sycophancy is a reliability failure and an ethical one; a low-confidence answer inside a high-stakes topic is not merely uncertain, it is something that can hurt someone. Forcing one detector to one meaning made the taxonomy look arbitrary, because it was.

Detectors stayed many and overlapping. What the user is told collapsed to a small set of concerns, and a single turn may raise several — the interface names the dominant one and lists the rest. It never names a filter.

The part worth keeping

Those four turns could have been given a plausible-sounding reason. Inventing one would have been precisely the failure this system exists to catch — a confident label with nothing behind it — so they carry an honest label instead, and its wording says the evidence is weaker than a named reason.

The first measured run also produced two alerts that could name no concern at all. That was a real bug: the reliability score read raw dispersion while the band was driven by the topic-amplified value. Fixed, then re-measured.

v22milestone 4

An ethics review of the prototype's own behaviour

Reviewed as an artefact rather than as a design, four problems surfaced that a functional review would not have raised. Two of them had been introduced earlier the same day.

Crisis help sat behind a tap.

Everywhere else in this system the rule is pull, not push — the explanation waits until asked for. Applied to a crisis turn, that rule put the most important content at the end of the longest path, behind an icon whose meaning the reader has to already know. Verified contact numbers now render inline in the conversation, once, with nothing to dismiss.

Indirect phrasing never crossed the line.

The flow written specifically to test veiled expressions of distress peaks at 35 and stays silent throughout. Say it plainly and the system reacts; hint at it and nothing happens — and the people most likely to hint are the ones it can least afford to miss. A softer tier now makes the support panel reachable without forcing an alert or claiming to know what the person means.

A concern that could harm the person it fires on.

A detector added for one-sided accounts of interpersonal conflict tells the reader that the other person is not present to give their version. Reasonable for an argument with a friend. Said to someone describing abuse, it questions their account on behalf of the absent party — which is among the best documented ways people are talked out of trusting themselves. The concern is now suppressed entirely when abuse markers are present, not softened.

The demo asserted work it had not done.

The waiting indicator read checking it against two more tries. True in live mode. In demo mode the three answers are pre-written and only the comparison is real — so the sentence claimed a process that had not run, inside a prototype whose entire subject is confident claims about work that was not done. It now distinguishes the two.

v23milestone 5

A researcher's vocabulary, surviving in the first thing anyone saw

The empty state offers three suggestion chips. One of them was labelled Fabricated drug — the name of the trap, written by the person who set it, sitting on the screen for the person walking into it.

It is the language of the test harness, not the language of someone opening a chat. It also gives the demonstration away: a chip that announces the answer will be invented turns a signal that has to be noticed into a result already known. It now reads Ask it to decide for you, which is what a person would actually be doing.

The first chip had a second fault underneath the wording. It carried no scripted samples of its own, so it fell through to a generic fallback — the one path in the empty state that answered with something other than its own content. It now has its own.

Why the last one still signalsThe three answers agree perfectly. The band is driven by the domain multiplier, not by dispersion — agreement is not evidence of safety.

Why it survived twenty-three builds

This was the first thing anyone saw of the prototype, and it had been there from the beginning. Every review until this one asked whether the thing worked: whether the classifier fired, whether the bands held, whether the panel said something defensible. All of those questions are answered inside the conversation, which is where the attention went.

It surfaced only when the prototype was read the way a designer reads it — starting at the empty state, from the position of someone who has never seen it before and does not know what a fabricated drug is for.

Three times the measurement overruled the design notes

These are recorded because they are the part that transfers. Each one was an assertion written confidently enough that it survived several revisions before anything was run.

A flow described as silent was reaching alert

Written up as a case where correct model behaviour produces no signal. Measured peak: 71, alert on turn two. Read from the transcript, never executed.

Two layers disagreed about the same turn

The scoring layer ranked sycophancy above one-sided framing; the explanation layer ranked them the other way. The same turn was labelled one thing and explained as another. Branch order drifted between two functions written a few hours apart — three times over the sequence.

A user-supplied false premise suppresses the detector

When the false claim originates in the prompt, the three samples converge on it and dispersion collapses — the signal is weakest exactly where the fabrication is most persuasive. The direction is robust across phrasings; the magnitude is not, and should not be quoted as a fixed figure until it is measured on live model output rather than written samples.
