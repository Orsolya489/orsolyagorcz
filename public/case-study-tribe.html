Case study 01

Building enough safety for someone to type the thing they actually want.

A social app where one prompt replaces the profile. The whole product rests on a single text field — and the trust required to use it honestly.

RoleLead designer
TypeAI · MVP
StatusPre-launch
DisciplinesAI Product · Human-AI Interaction · Intent Search

Overview

Connect strangers through intent, not profiles.

Tribe connects people through a single prompt — not a profile, not a browse interface. The user types what they want to do, the AI matches them with people looking for the same thing, and connection happens before either party knows who the other is.

The entire product hangs on one text field. Making someone feel safe enough to be honest in it was the design problem — not the matching algorithm.

Problem framing

Current platforms prioritise short-term attention. The gap: long-term trust.

The brief was an intent-based social network for Budapest. The real brief, once I'd mapped the competitive landscape, was: how do you get someone to type what they actually want — not a curated version of it — into a field connected to a stranger-matching algorithm?

That's a trust problem before it's a product problem. It shaped every design decision that followed.

Solutions

Full IA. Not everything survived the dev sync.

Three generations of input validation designed — two cut after synching with developers on latency, token cost, and structural reliability. What shipped earned it.

The rest of the architecture — match logic, processing states, and safety exits — was built around the system that survived.

Gen 1 — cut

Manual tags

Freeform. No structure. System can't match on noise.

No chat search, one active conversation. The constraint only works because the exits are designed in — without them, a single-chat system becomes a trap.

A deliberate bet against dopamine loops — no infinite scroll, no variable reward. One thing to care about, not a feed to scroll.

Reflection & next steps

What I'd do differently. What I'd do the same.

Send a questionnaire before any screen was drawn. Real user intent data would have sharpened the input validation from day one.

What I'd do the same

Push back on anything that doesn't serve the user. Every constraint that survived the dev sync survived because it had a human reason behind it.

If this shipped tomorrow

One metric: prompt quality in correlation to match quality. That correlation — or its absence — would tell me whether the core thesis holds.

Proposed testing plans · alpha phase

Alpha validation blueprint

Phase 1 — pre-launch lab testing · usability

Objective

Verify if real-time keyword highlighting reduces input anxiety and ensures semantic accuracy.

Methodology

Moderated testing (8–10 participants) using low-fidelity click-dummies.

Signals

Time-on-task, token correction frequency, and subjective workload (SEQ score).

Phase 2 — alpha field testing · telemetry

Objective

Validate the NLP matching engine and progressive disclosure mechanics under live infrastructure load.

Methodology

14-day unmoderated field test deployed to a high-density cohort of 500–1,000 target users in Budapest.

Signals

Progress toward the target ≥40% match-to-meetup conversion milestone, live data logging against the <40% engine failure threshold, and exact drop-off points within the unmasking sequence.
